I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at [Michigan State University](https://msu.edu), where I work in the [iProbe Lab](https://iprobe.cse.msu.edu/) advised by [Dr. Arun Ross](https://www.cse.msu.edu/~rossarun/). My research sits at the intersection of audio deepfake detection, speaker identity bias, adversarial robustness in speech systems, and explainability in biometric systems.

Broadly, I'm interested in a question that keeps coming up in my work: when a detector says "fake" or "real," how much of that decision is actually about synthesis artifacts, and how much is it quietly leaning on *who* is speaking? Most of my projects — from measuring identity sensitivity in deepfake detectors to studying how adversarial perturbations shift speaker identity at the phonetic level — try to pull that question apart and build tools that make these systems more reliable and more explainable.

I have papers accepted at ICASSP 2026 (oral) and IJCB 2026, with a third currently under review at SLT 2026. My current project builds a contrastive, speaker-normalized system for generating acoustic explanations of deepfake detector decisions.

Outside of research, I spend time thinking about applied and entrepreneurial ideas in voice AI and agentic tools, and I game on a dual-GPU setup when I get the chance.

Feel free to reach out via email if you'd like to chat about audio forensics, biometrics, or speech ML.
